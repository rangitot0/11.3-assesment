Lesson 4 - Microorganisms

What they are

A microorganism is a living thing thats too small to see, so you need a microscope. Bacteria, fungi and protists. They live pretty much everywhere, soil, water, your skin, your gut.

Bacteria cell

The parts

Slime capsule - stops it drying out and helps it stick to things

Cell wall - gives it its shape and stops it bursting

Cell membrane - controls what goes in and out

Cytoplasm - where the reactions happen

Genetic material - a loose loop of DNA, not in a nucleus

Flagella - tail that whips around to move it

Binary fission

This is how bacteria reproduce. The DNA gets copied, the cell grows, then it splits into two identical cells. Its asexual so theres no variation, but its fast.

Extracellular digestion

Bacteria and fungi cant swallow, so they digest outised!!! They excrete enzymes out onto the food source, the enzymes break it down and then the small bits get digested back in. "Extra" means outside.

Enzymes

Enzymes speed reactions up without getting used up. They have a specific shape so each one only fits one substrate.

If its too hot or the wrong pH the enzyme denatures. The shape changes for good and it stops working.

Respiration equations

Learn these ones exactly

Aerobic: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water (+ energy)

Anaerobic in bacteria: glucose → lactic acid (+ energy)

Anaerobic in fungi: glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide (+ energy)

The difference is oxygen. Aerobic uses it and gets alot of energy. Anaerobic dos not so it doesnt produce as much energy. .

Fermentation

Yeast is a fungus. In bread the carbon dioxide makes the dough rise (fermentation). In beer the ethanol is what they extract (product of fermentation).
